One-paragraph dek: what this object is and why the Muse stopped here.

## Historical Context

The world that made it — and, for finds, the world that dug it up (the story
of a discovery is often as load-bearing as the object).

## The Object

What it physically is: material, inscription, iconography, condition,
provenance chain. What is actually known versus inferred.

## What It Shows

The reading of it — translation of inscriptions, interpretation of imagery —
with interpreter named and dated. Interpretation is a claim; treat it as one.

## Claims & Disputes

Archaeology and art attract dating disputes, forgery questions, and
speculative readings more than any other node type. Tier everything:
**Established / Contested / Tradition / Speculation** — steelman then source.
