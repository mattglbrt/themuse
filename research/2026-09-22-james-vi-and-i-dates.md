## Old Style / New Style notes (for the node author)

- **Year start.** England began the year on 25 March until 1752. Scotland switched to 1 January from 1600, by Privy Council ordinance of 17 December 1599 (rows 29–30). The accession proclamation is printed "March, 24. Anno Domini, 1602" (row 28). Modern style: **24 March 1603 (1602 O.S. in England)**. A Scottish record of the same day would already say 1603.
- **The Kronborg ceremony.** 21 January 1590 (row 22) fell before Scotland's 1600 change, while Scotland still began the year on 25 March (row 29). A Scottish record of that day would therefore probably read January 1589. *(inferred from rows 22 and 29; no fetched document shows it.)* The Oslo wedding (23 Nov 1589) and the landing at Leith (1 May 1590) fall outside the 1 January to 24 March window and are unaffected.
- **Birth (19 June 1566) and death (27 March 1625)** both fall outside the window. No year ambiguity.
- **Julian vs Gregorian.** All dates here are as the sources print them. No fetched source in this session states which calendar (Julian or Gregorian) Scotland, England or Denmark used in 1566–1625, so the memo does not assert it. That is an open item. If the page is to give Gregorian equivalents, research them separately first.

## Conflicts a node author must handle

1. **Proxy marriage month.** The DNB (both articles) says 20 Aug 1589. Of the two contemporary chroniclers, one says July (rows 9–10). Suggested line: "married by proxy at Copenhagen on 20 August 1589 (DNB; one contemporary annalist says July)."
2. **James's sailing.** DNB, the anonymous contemporary and a declaration dated at Leith all give 22 October. Marjoribanks gives the "penult day" (30th). Use 22 October and footnote the variant.
3. **Which wedding.** There were several. The Copenhagen proxy (Aug 1589), the wedding in person at Oslo (23 Nov 1589; DNB, Moysie, the 24 Nov morning-gift grant) and a further ceremony in Denmark ("marriet of new", Marjoribanks; 21 Jan 1590 at Kronborg per Westminster Abbey only). Westminster Abbey's page names only Kronborg. Do not cite it alone for "the" wedding date.
4. **English coronation: 25 July (WA) vs 24 July (Ward, DNB).** Unresolved. The Gardiner DNB article gives no date. Needs a print check or a primary account.
5. **Burial: 5 May (DNB) vs funeral 17 May (WA).** These may be different events (interment vs funeral), but no fetched source says so. Unresolved. Needs a print check.

## Source-quality notes

- **Primary:** *Diurnal of Occurrents* (a 16th-c. Scottish chronicle, Bannatyne Club ed. 1833). The Bannatyne Club *Papers relative to the Marriage* (1828) prints state papers (the Leith declaration, the Upslo morning-gift grant, the 1590 coronation account) and quotes three contemporary narratives: an annalist the editor identifies as George Marjoribanks, an "unknown historian", and David Moysie's *Memoirs*. The narratives are primary but disagree in detail (rows 9–10, 13–15). The accession proclamation (EEBO microfilm scan) is primary.
- **Scholarly secondary (Victorian):** DNB, "James VI and I" by S. R. Gardiner, and "Anne of Denmark" by A. W. Ward. Both are signed articles by major historians. On the voyage, Ward's article plainly follows the Bannatyne material (the same Slaikray, 19 Nov and Lyndsay details). So on the Oslo wedding it is not independent of row 18. The Wikisource page for the Anne article is "Proofread", not validated.
- **Edited record:** *Register of the Privy Council of Scotland* vol. VI, ed. David Masson (1884). The editor's introduction summarises an ordinance printed in the volume. The ordinance text itself (said to be at p. 638) was not fetched.
- **Institutional web page:** Westminster Abbey. It is authoritative for its own burials but unsigned and unreferenced, and on two points (coronation day, funeral day) it disagrees with the DNB.
- **Tertiary:** EB1911 (rows 4, 36). Kept only as corroboration and to document its death-date error. It does not count toward the two-source rule.
- **OCR caveat:** the archive.org quotes are the OCR as returned (long-s read as "f", "Majestic" for "Majestie", "parifli" for "parish"). Anyone quoting these on the site should check the page image and normalise openly.
