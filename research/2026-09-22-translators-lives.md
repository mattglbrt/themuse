# Research memo: lives of the KJV translators and printer (Andrewes, Bancroft, Rainolds, Miles Smith, Robert Barker)

**Date:** 2026-09-22 · **Slug:** translators-lives · **Log:** `research/logs/2026-09-22-translators-lives.log.md` · **Sources JSON:** `research/sources/2026-09-22-translators-lives.json`

## The question

From `research/QUEUE.md` (Following the Muse): for Lancelot Andrewes, Richard Bancroft, John Rainolds, Miles Smith and Robert Barker, find birth and death dates and places, education, main offices with dates, and each man's part in the KJV. For Barker, also his later career and death. The pages need dates, offices and KJV role only, not full biographies. Find a second independent source for each birth and death date, flag Old Style dates and report conflicts between sources.

## Headline findings

- **Andrewes.** Born 1555 in the parish of All Hallows, Barking, London (DNB, ODNB title, Venn, Isaacson). Merchant Taylors' School, then Pembroke Hall, Cambridge: fellow 1576, Master 1589–1605. Dean of Westminster 1601–05 (installed 4 July 1601). Bishop of Chichester 1605 (consecrated 3 Nov. 1605), of Ely 1609 and of Winchester 1619. Buried at St Saviour's, Southwark (now Southwark Cathedral). His name stands first in the 1607 list of translators, in the first Westminster company (Genesis–2 Kings). **Conflict on the day of death:** the DNB says 26 Sept. 1626. Isaacson (his amanuensis, 1650), Laud's diary (as cited by the modern *Fasti*), Venn and Westminster Abbey all say **25 Sept. 1626**. The weight of evidence favours 25 September. **Place of death: not found.**
- **Bancroft.** Born at Farnworth, Lancashire, in September 1544. Christ's College, Cambridge (B.A. 1566-7 in Old Style). Bishop of London 1597 (elected 21 Apr., consecrated 8 May). Archbishop of Canterbury 1604 (elected 17 Nov., confirmed 10 Dec.). Chancellor of Oxford 1608. Died 2 Nov. 1610 and buried at Lambeth. At Hampton Court he opposed a new translation ("no end of translating"). Once the King backed it, he supported it. **Minor conflict:** the DNB puts his London enthronement on 5 June, the *Fasti* on 6 June 1597. **Birth rests on one source:** Venn repeats the DNB and cites it. **Place of death: not found.**
- **Rainolds.** Born at Pinhoe, near Exeter, "about Michaelmas Day" 1549 (DNB; the year is also in the ODNB title). Corpus Christi College, Oxford: scholar 29 Apr. 1563, fellow 1566, B.A. 15 Oct. 1568, Greek reader 1572–3. Dean of Lincoln (elected 10 Dec. 1593, instituted 28 Mar. 1594), which he exchanged in 1598 for the presidency of Corpus. Died 21 May 1607 and buried in the college chapel. At Hampton Court he proposed the translation (see the hampton-court memo). He sat in the Oxford company for the Prophets. **Conflict on age at death:** the DNB says "not yet fifty-eight", Foster "aged 58".
- **Miles Smith.** **Birth date not found.** Neither the DNB nor Foster gives one, and the DNB heads the article "(d. 1624)". Born at Hereford. **Conflict on his father's trade:** "a butcher" (DNB) against "a fletcher's son" (Foster). Corpus Christi, then Brasenose: B.A. 1573, M.A. 1576, B.D. 1585, D.D. 1594. Canon of Hereford 1580. Translated the Prophets. With Bilson he made the final revision, and he wrote the preface. Bishop of Gloucester, consecrated 20 Sept. **Conflict on the year of consecration:** the DNB and Foster give 1612. The BHO transcription of the modern *Fasti* dates the process to 1611, but the same page records his predecessor Tomson dying in June 1612, so the *Fasti* text contradicts itself. This needs a print check. Died 20 Oct. 1624 and buried in Gloucester Cathedral on 9 Nov. 1624 (Foster only).
- **Robert Barker.** **Birth date not found.** Plomer says he was "probably" born near Datchet, Bucks; the OCR of the place name is unclear. He was the son of Christopher Barker. Free of the Stationers' Company 25 June 1589. He held the reversion of the Queen's Printer's patent (8 Aug. 1589), recognised by the Stationers on 3 Jan. 1599–1600 (Old Style; 1600 New Style). He held the office under James and was Master of the Stationers in 1605 and 1606. He printed the 1611 first edition (two folio issues). Later career:
  - a long fight with Bonham Norton and John Bill for control of the King's Printing House (**conflict on dating:** DNB "1627" against Plomer's assignment soon after 1615 and a Chancery suit in 1618);
  - the fine over the 1631 "Wicked Bible";
  - a mortgage of his share in 1634;
  - committed to the King's Bench prison on 27 Nov. 1635, where he stayed until his death on **10 Jan. 1644–5** (Old Style; 1645 New Style). The day is DNB only; Plomer gives the year 1645.

## Conflicts between sources (reported, not resolved)

1. **Andrewes' date of death:** the DNB says 26 Sept. 1626. Isaacson (1650, his amanuensis), the *Fasti* (citing Laud's diary), Venn and Westminster Abbey say 25 Sept. 1626. Venn cites the DNB yet prints the 25th. Recommendation: use 25 Sept. and note the DNB's 26th.
2. **Bancroft's London enthronement:** 5 June 1597 (DNB) against 6 June 1597 (*Fasti*, citing Guildhall MS). Minor.
3. **Rainolds' age at death:** "not yet fifty-eight" (DNB) against "aged 58" (Foster).
4. **Miles Smith's father:** a butcher (DNB) against a fletcher (Foster).
5. **Miles Smith's consecration year:** 1612 (DNB, Foster) against 1611 (BHO *Fasti* text, which contradicts itself). Needs a print check of the *Fasti*.
6. **Barker and Norton/Bill:** assignment in 1627 (DNB) against soon after 1615, with litigation from 1618 (Plomer).
7. **Rainolds' deanery start:** elected 10 Dec. 1593 (DNB, *Fasti*) against "1594-9" (Foster). Explained by his institution on 28 Mar. 1594, so this is not a real conflict.

## Source-quality notes

- **Primary:** Isaacson's *Life and Death of Lancelot Andrewes*. He was Andrewes' amanuensis and friend, and the 1829 reprint says the Life was first printed in 1650. It is the strongest source for Andrewes' death date. A friend wrote it, so use it for dates, not for judgements.
- **Scholarly secondary (modern, archival):** *Fasti Ecclesiae Anglicanae 1541–1857* (on British History Online). It cites the registers (Lambeth, PRO patent rolls, diocesan act books) for each step and is the best source for office dates. The BHO transcription of the Smith entry looks internally inconsistent (row 37b), so check it against print.
- **Scholarly reference (Victorian/Edwardian):** DNB 1885–1900 (Overton, Mullinger, Fowler, Carlyle, Tedder). Foster, *Alumni Oxonienses* (1891–2). Venn, *Alumni Cantabrigienses* (the ACAD database). Plomer, *Dictionary* (Bibliographical Society, 1907). These are not fully independent: **Venn cites the DNB** in both entries used. Foster draws on Wood's *Athenae* ("Ath."). Plomer draws on his own 1901 *Library* article and the Chancery records. Where Venn only repeats the DNB (Bancroft's birth), I have not counted it as a second source.
- **ODNB (2004):** the article pages return 403. Only the Crossref titles (lifespans) for Andrewes and Rainolds were usable. The Crossref "odnb/9780192683120.013.*" records (Bancroft, Smith, Barker) are the digitised old DNB. They are not independent and were not used.
- **Institutional web page:** the Westminster Abbey commemoration page for Andrewes is tertiary but corroborates.
- **OCR:** the Foster (archive.org) and Plomer quotes are OCR as printed ("si May" = 21 May, "bom" = born, "commined" = committed, "161 1" = 1611). Plomer's "October zoth, 1639" was too garbled to use.
