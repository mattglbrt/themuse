Andrew Downes sat on the second Cambridge company of King James translators, the one given the Apocrypha, with his pupil John Bois. John Selden's *Table-Talk* uses him as the example of how the work was shared out: each part "was given to him who was most excellent in such a tongue (as the Apocrypha to Andrew Downs)". His Greek letters to [[isaac-casaubon]] are in the British Library, which makes him the second translator, after Lancelot Andrewes, documented in contact with Casaubon.

## Where the Muse Leads

- [[isaac-casaubon]]: Downes wrote to him in Greek. It's a scholar's correspondence, not evidence of Hermetic learning on the translation.
- [[king-james-bible]]: his company translated the Apocrypha for it.
