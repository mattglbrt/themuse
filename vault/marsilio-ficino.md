Marsilio Ficino made the Latin translation of the Hermetic writings, finished in 1463 and printed at Treviso in 1471 as *Pimander*, "Aus dem Griech. übers. von Marsilius Ficinus". Brian Copenhaver says those writings "aren't about magic, and Ficino understood that." He's a seed here because the usual story of a Renaissance "Hermetic Tradition" begins with his translation, a story Wouter Hanegraaff argues needs revising.

## Where the Muse Leads

- [[corpus-hermeticum]]: the texts he translated.
- [[hermeticism]]: the "Hermetic Tradition" long said to begin with his translation, and now questioned.
