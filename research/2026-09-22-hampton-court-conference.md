# Research memo: The Hampton Court Conference (1604), the Millenary Petition, and the Geneva notes

**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Question (Phase 1, KJV node, Q1 of 8):** The Millenary Petition: its date, content, and who presented it. Who proposed a new translation at Hampton Court, and how did James I respond? Why were the Geneva Bible's marginal notes a political problem for him? Find the primary record (William Barlow, *The Summe and Substance of the Conference*, 1604) and scholarly secondary sources. Quote James's recorded remarks on the Geneva notes exactly as printed.

**Short answer:**
- **The petition.** Gee & Hardy (1896) say it "was presented to James on his way to London after his accession" and date it April 1603. A second reference work agrees. The text claims "more than a thousand" ministers. Its requests are about ceremonies, ministers, livings and discipline, and it asks for the matters to be settled "by conference among the learned". Who actually handed it over is thinly sourced. The 1885-1900 DNB names Stephen Egerton as "one of those chosen to present" it and calls Arthur Hildersam one of its "most active managers". The same DNB also dates the presentation wrongly.
- **The proposal.** Barlow's own text (EEBO-TCP A04434, 1604 imprint) has Dr Reynolds (John Rainolds, the puritans' "Foreman") move "that there might bee a newe translation of the Bible". The Bishop of London (Richard Bancroft) answered that "there would be no ende of translating". James then called for "one vniforme translation", said "the worst of all, his Maiestie thought the *Geneua* to bee", and forbade marginal notes.
- **The Geneva notes.** Barlow records James saying he had found "some notes very partiall, vntrue, seditious, and sauouring too much, of daungerous, and trayterous conceites". James gave two examples: Exod. 1:19 (a note that "alloweth disobedience to Kings") and 2 Chron. 15:16 (a note that "taxeth Asa for deposing his mother, onely, and not killing her"). A second digitization (the 1804 reprint) gives the same wording.
- **Why it mattered.** James's own *Trew Law* (1598) denies that subjects may lawfully "shake off" a wicked king. Tying that to the notes is analysis, not a quotation. Furniss (2009) confirms that the Geneva Bible has long been read as "revolutionary or seditious" because of its notes, but he argues that the notes' politics "are undecidable". So "the Geneva notes were seditious" is **Contested**. "James said they were seditious" is **Established**.

## Source-quality notes

- **Primary (strongest):** Barlow 1604 via EEBO-TCP A04434 (rows 12, 14, 16-18, 22-26). It is a transcription keyed from the 1604 print, with the imprint visible in the header. The 1804 reprint on archive.org (row 27) is a second, separate digitization of the same text and matches. The 1611 "Translators to the Reader" (row 21) is primary but partisan. The Millenary Petition text (rows 4-8) is primary but reaches us through Fuller (1655), then Gee & Hardy (1896), then Hanover. It is not a manuscript witness. *Trew Law* (row 30) is primary, in McIlwain's 1918 scholarly edition.
- **Barlow is a partisan source.** He was Dean of Chester and wrote with official approval. Quote him *as Barlow's record*: "According to Barlow's semi-official account, James said..." The quotations of James are what Barlow printed, not a verbatim transcript. The Curtis/Shriver debate (rows 33-35) is the node's "Contested" paragraph. Barlow's own asides ("well added", "triuiall and old") are editorializing and should be presented that way.
- **Scholarly secondary:** Furniss 2009 (peer-reviewed, abstract only). Shriver 1982 (peer-reviewed, extract only). Curtis 1961 (not accessed). This is the weakest part of the memo. The Geneva-notes-as-politics point has the primary text plus one scholarly abstract.
- **Tertiary:** the old DNB (1885-1900, signed articles by Fowler, Mullinger, Vian and Gordon). It is good for identities and dates, but it is dated and in one case (row 11) wrong on the petition's date. Lockyer (*Oxford Companion*) and the CCEL encyclopedia. The CCEL page does not show its encyclopedia name; the identification comes from the URL path.
- **Weak (use with a flag):** the Geneva note texts (rows 28-29) come from modernized online transcriptions of the 1599 notes, not a facsimile. Before any page quotes the Geneva wording, someone should read the 1560 facsimile (archive.org genevabiblefacsi0000unse, University of Wisconsin Press 1969, which this session could not text-search) or the 1599 edition. The Craig blog is by a credentialed church historian but it is a blog. Use it as a map to Curtis, the Harleian account and Burntisland.
- **The Geneva edition James saw is unknown.** Barlow says only "a Bible giuen him by an English Lady". Don't state which edition.
