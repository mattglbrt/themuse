# Research memo: the General Assembly at Burntisland, May 1601

**Date:** 2026-09-22 · **Slug:** burntisland-1601 · **Log:** `research/logs/2026-09-22-burntisland-1601.log.md` · **Sources:** `research/sources/2026-09-22-burntisland-1601.json`

## The question

Did James VI urge a new Bible translation at the General Assembly of the Church of Scotland held at Burntisland in May 1601? Quote the Assembly record (*Booke of the Universall Kirk* / *Acts and Proceedings*, Bannatyne and Maitland Clubs, 1839-45), Spottiswoode (1655 and the 19th-century edition) and Calderwood (Wodrow Society), with volume and page. Record what the Assembly actually resolved and whether anything came of it. This follows up HCC memo row 36 (`research/2026-09-22-hampton-court-conference.md`), which had this second-hand only.

## Headline

1. **The Assembly register does not say James urged it.** The official record (*Booke of the Universall Kirk*, Part III, p. 970) attributes the motion to "sundrie of the brethren", in the last session (16 May). The King is recorded as present at the Assembly (p. 963), but the translation item names no speaker and mentions no royal speech. Calderwood's *History* (vi. 124) gives the same wording, and he doesn't credit James either.
2. **James's urging rests on one witness: John Spottiswoode.** His *History of the Church of Scotland* (1655, p. 465; 19th-century edition iii. 98-99) says "His majesty did urge it earnestly", that James pointed out "sundry escapes in the common translation", and that he recited Psalm verses to show the faults of the metre. Later writers (Westcott 1911, Craig 2011) depend on Spottiswoode. No second, independent witness was found.
3. **What was resolved.** Ministers skilled in the languages were to work on the parts of the "vulgar translatioun" that needed mending and compare their work at the Assembly. Robert Pont was to revise the metrical Psalms, and his work was to be reviewed at the next Assembly. The existing prayers in the Psalm Book were not to be changed, but new prayers could be added once the Assembly had tried and approved them.
4. **Nothing came of it in Scotland.** Spottiswoode says "nothing was done in the one or the other". Craig (2011) reports no mention at the following Assembly. A full-text search of Part III found no later entry on the Bible revision (a negative search, not proof). Spottiswoode links the episode to the English translation James set going after 1603, and to James's own Psalm paraphrase.
5. **Page-number conflict.** In the scanned 1839 volume (Part III) the item is on p. 970 (running head "970 THE BOOKE OF THE 1601."). British History Online's transcription, which Craig cites, puts the same text under "Appendix: 1601, May", between its page markers 1083 and 1084. **Cite p. 970 from the scan.** The BHO pagination is unexplained and needs a print check.

## What the node author can say (summary in plain terms)

- **Safe to state:** At the Burntisland Assembly (12-16 May 1601, King present), the Kirk resolved to have its language-skilled ministers mend the "vulgar" English translation and compare their work at the Assembly, and had Robert Pont revise the metrical Psalms. Nothing came of it in Scotland.
- **State with attribution:** "According to John Spottiswoode, later archbishop of St Andrews, the King 'did urge it earnestly'..." The register credits the motion to "sundrie of the brethren", and Calderwood follows the register. James's personal advocacy rests on Spottiswoode alone.
- **Not supported:** that the official record shows James proposing a translation, or that the Scottish Assembly commissioned a new Bible. It commissioned corrections to the existing "vulgar translatioun", which the record does not identify. Don't equate it with the Geneva Bible without a source.
- **Use for Hampton Court:** this is a plausible precursor, framed as Spottiswoode frames it. Spottiswoode wrote after 1603 with the King James Bible already in view, and his book was published in 1655, after his death.

## Source-quality notes

- **Primary (register):** *The Booke of the Universall Kirk of Scotland* / *Acts and Proceedings*, Part III (Bannatyne and Maitland Clubs, 1839, ed. Thomas Thomson). The scan's leaf 18 minute confirms joint printing "for the use, of the Members of the Bannatyne and Maitland Clubs". Two independent scans of the Bannatyne issue v. 3 (Princeton, actsproceedingso03chur; Google, actsandproceedi03assegoog) both carry the text. The printed page, 970, is confirmed by the running head. The index lists "Bible ... translation of, 970", but the errata leaf reads "Bible, for 970, read 992". That erratum is ambiguous: p. 992 is the 1602 book-list article (row 21), and the text of the 1601 item is certainly on p. 970.
- **Primary (narrative, partisan on both sides):** Spottiswoode (1565-1639, dates from the archive.org creator field) was the pro-episcopal historian and later archbishop. Calderwood (1575-1650) was a Presbyterian opponent of the Crown's church policy. They disagree about James's role: Spottiswoode gives him the initiative and Calderwood follows the register. Whether that difference reflects their politics is a fair question, but no scholar found this session says so. **Don't assert a motive.**
- **Independence:** Calderwood's account is almost word-for-word the register (rows 4-9), so it does not independently confirm the resolutions. It does show that a second contemporary historian did not report James urging the work. Westcott, the DNB and Craig all depend on Spottiswoode for James's role.
- **Transcriptions:** BHO (double-rekeyed, stated "Public Domain") is a clean reading text, but its page markers conflict with the print (row 20). Quote BHO for readable spelling if wanted, and cite the print page from the scan.
- **Scholarly secondary:** none on point was reachable. Craig is a credentialed church historian writing on a blog. Fincham, "The King James Bible: Crown, Church and People", *JEH* 71.1 (2020) 77-97 (https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0022046918001318/type/journal_article), is abstract only and says nothing about Burntisland in the accessible text. It is listed here as further reading only.
- Craig also says Norton quotes Spottiswoode but omits the "nothing was done" sentence. Norton's text was not fetched, so that claim is not in the table.
