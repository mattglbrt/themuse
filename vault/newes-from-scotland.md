*Newes from Scotland* is a London pamphlet reporting the examinations of people accused in the [[north-berwick-witch-trials|North Berwick witch hunt]], "as they vttered them in the presence of the Scottish king." The Muse stops here because it is the closest thing to an eyewitness account of [[james-i|King James]] questioning the accused himself. It is also propaganda, built on confessions taken under torture, and it has to be read that way.

## Historical Context

The pamphlet reports on the hunt of 1590-91, in which James was "involved as alleged victim, interrogator, judge, and demonologist" (Normand & Roberts 2000, abstract). Normand and Roberts, who edit the standard scholarly edition, write that "News from Scotland was the first publication in Scotland and England that reported exclusively on Scottish witchcraft. It was also the most propagandastic of all the texts generated by the North Berwick witch trials" ("propagandastic" is their spelling; chapter "Witch Hunt Propaganda: News from Scotland," pp. 290-326, abstract).

## The Document

It was printed in London for William Wright. The printer and the exact year are not certain. The EEBO-TCP transcription's header gives the imprint as "Printed [By E. Allde?] for William Wright," (STC 10841A), and the Wellcome Collection record gives "At London : Printed [By E. Allde?] for William Wright, [1592?]" with the note "Printer's name and publication date conjectured by STC." The title page itself says 1591. Both records go back to STC and the same Bodleian copy, so they are not two independent witnesses.

The best free text is the EEBO-TCP transcription A00710 of the Bodleian copy, released under CC0.

## Contents

Read as the pamphlet's account, not as a record of events:

- **How it started.** In Tranent, "there dwelleth one Dauid Seaton, who being deputie Bailiffe in the saide Towne, had a maide seruant called Geillis Duncane." He had her tortured: "torment her with the torture of the Pilliwinckes vpon her fingers." Under that torture [[geillis-duncan|Geillis Duncan]] named others, first among them "Agnis Sampson the eldest Witch of them al, dwelling in Haddington."
- **The king's examination.** [[agnes-sampson|Agnes Sampson]] "was taken and brought to Haliriud house before the Kings Maiestie and sundry other of the nobility of Scotland, where she was straitly examined." The pamphlet says that "his Maiestie saide they were all extreame lyars," and that she then "declared vnto him the verye woordes which passed betweene the Kings Maiestie and his Queene at Upslo in Norway the first night of their mariage."
- **What was confessed.** The pamphlet reports confessions of a gathering with "the Diuell being then at North Barrick Kerke attending their comming in the habit or likenes of a man," and that "the said christened Cat was the cause that the Kinges Maiesties Ship at his comming foorth of Denmarke, had a contrary winde to the rest of his Ships." Its full title says the accused tried to bewitch and drown the king at sea on his way home from Denmark.
- **Doctor Fian.** It names "Doctor Fian, alias Iohn Cunningham, maister of the Schoole at Saltpans in Lowthian," describes his torture in "the bootes," and says he was "burned in the Castle hill of Edenbrough on asaterdaie in the ende of Ianuarie last past. 1591." See [[john-fian]].

(All quotations: *Newes from Scotland*, EEBO-TCP A00710.)

## Claims & Disputes

- **Contested: who wrote it.** The pamphlet is anonymous. The Wellcome Collection record says it is "Attributed to James Carmichael." That is a cataloguer's attribution, and this page treats authorship as open.
- **Contested: the year.** The title page says 1591; STC conjectures [1592?].
- **Established, as confession content only:** the Devil at North Berwick kirk, the christened cat, and the plot against the king's ship are what the accused were reported to have confessed. The pamphlet itself names the pilliwinks and the boots, so the conditions of those confessions are on its own pages. What can be stated flatly is that confessions were made, by named people, under torture, in front of the king.

## Where the Muse Leads

- [[north-berwick-witch-trials]]: the hunt this pamphlet was written to report.
- [[daemonologie]]: the king's own treatise on witchcraft, printed in 1597; the standard modern edition prints the two together.
- [[james-i]]: the pamphlet's central figure, questioning the accused at Holyrood.
- [[agnes-sampson]]: the woman whose examination before the king is the pamphlet's centrepiece.
- [[john-fian]]: the schoolmaster whose torture and death the pamphlet describes.
- [[geillis-duncan]]: the servant whose torture started the chain of accusations.
