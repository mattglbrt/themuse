William Barlow wrote *[[summe-and-substance-of-the-conference|The Summe and Substance of the Conference]]* (1604), the printed account of the [[hampton-court-conference|Hampton Court Conference]]. The Muse stops here because nearly every word we have from that day, including James's attack on the Geneva notes, is Barlow's record. A William Barlow, Dean of Chester, also stands at the head of one of the six companies that made the [[king-james-bible]].

## Historical Context

The Hampton Court Conference met in January 1604 to hear the puritan complaints set out in the [[millenary-petition]]. Barlow's book came out the same year, "Printed by Iohn Windet, for Mathew Law, 1604" (EEBO-TCP A04434). The translation it records was organized in six companies, "two each in Westminster, Oxford and Cambridge" (Cambridge University Library 2011).

## Life & Work

**The account.** The *Summe and Substance* is the source for Rainolds's request for "a newe translation of the Bible," Bancroft's retort that "there would be no ende of translating," and James's call for "one vniforme translation" (Barlow 1604). Barlow isn't a neutral reporter. He says Bancroft "well added" his objection, and dismisses Rainolds's examples as "triuiall and old." So his book is best read as one side's record of the conference.

**The translation.** The standard list of translators, printed by Gilbert Burnet and reprinted by A. W. Pollard in 1911, gives the second Westminster company the Epistles of St Paul and the canonical Epistles. First on that company's list is the Dean of Chester, whom Pollard identifies: "Dean of Chester : William Barlow, Fellow of Trinity Hall, Dean, 1602 ; Bishop of Rochester, 1605" (Pollard 1911). The other names on it are Hutchinson, Spencer, Fenton, Rabbett, Sanderson, and Dakins.

The translators' rules name the Dean of Chester as one of the directors at Westminster: "13. The Directors in each Company, to be the Deans of Westminster and Chester for that Place; and the King's Professors in the Hebrew or Greek in either University" (Burnet's text, via Pollard 1911). Who actually chaired each company's sittings is inferred from the rule and the list order, not recorded directly.

The sources quoted on this page don't state in one sentence that the author of the *Summe and Substance* and the Dean of Chester on the translator list are the same man. This page treats them as one William Barlow, and that identification still needs a quoted source.
