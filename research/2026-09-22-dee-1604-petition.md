# Dee's 1604 petition to James I, and his verse appeal to the Commons

## The question

From `research/QUEUE.md` (Following the Muse). Rows 13–14 of `2026-09-22-hermeticism-cabala-england.md` rest only on the DNB (1885–1900) "Dee, John". Establish:
(a) that the petition exists, with its date, place and exact key wording ("conjurer, or caller, or invocator of divels"), from the primary text if possible;
(b) the evidence for James's refusal and for Salisbury's role, and where the DNB got it;
(c) whether Dee also petitioned Parliament in 1604 in a separate document, so the pages don't conflate the two.

WebSearch was unavailable (quota exhausted). Everything below came from direct fetches: archive.org advancedsearch, metadata and in-book full-text search (`fulltext/inside.php`, which returns the raw OCR text of each hit), Wikisource, Crossref and OpenAlex.

## Headline findings

1. **(a) The petition exists as a printed 1604 broadside.** It is titled *To the Kings most excellent Maiestie* and was printed by E. Short (archive.org scan of the EEBO microfilm, row 1). The key wording is confirmed in two places. Ellis's 1843 transcription of the British Museum sheet reads "That he is, or hath bin a Conjurer, or Caller, or Invocator of divels". The OCR of the broadside scan itself has the same words, garbled ("hath bin a { onurer,or { aller,or I1vocator of duels"). **5 June 1604 at Greenwich** comes from the heading of Ellis's transcription ("exhibited: Anno 1604 Junij 5, at Greenewich"). Thomas Smith's Latin life (1707) independently gives June 1604 and delivery to King James. I could not read a date line in the broadside OCR (rows 2–3).
2. **(b) The Salisbury story has a traceable origin, and it is weak.** The DNB (Thompson Cooper) repeats Cooper's own *Athenae Cantabrigienses* vol. 2, so the two are not independent. The earliest source found is **Thomas Smith, *Vita Joannis Dee* (1707)**, a century after the event. Smith says the king was "informed" about Dee by "D. Roberto Cecilio, Comite Salisburiae, & Angliae Thesaurario" and, *ut videtur* ("as it seems"), despised him. Smith hedges it. He gives Cecil titles Cecil did not hold in June 1604: Cecil became Viscount Cranborne on 20 Aug 1604 and **Earl of Salisbury on 4 May 1605** (DNB "Cecil, Robert"; Fell Smith). Smith gives no document for it. Two other secondary authors do not report a Salisbury-informed refusal at all. Ellis (1843): "It probably failed to attract the notice he expected". Fell Smith (1909): "Nor did James respond in any way to his heartbroken petition" (rows 9–12). **Recommendation:** the pages should say the petition got nothing from the king. "The king refused it on Salisbury's information" should appear only as the account of Smith (1707), carried into Cooper and the DNB. It should not be stated as fact.
3. **(c) Yes, there are two separate 1604 documents.** Dee also printed a **verse** appeal, *To the honorable Assemblie of the Commons in the present Parlament* (same printer, E. Short). It is signed "Iohn Dee, Seruant and Mathematician to his most royall Maiestie" (OCR). Athenae Cantab. and Fell Smith both date it **8 June 1604**, three days after the petition to the king. The petition to the king also *mentions* Parliament: it asks to be tried before the king, the Privy Council, or "the present assembled Parlament States". Keep that request separate from the Commons verse broadside (rows 4, 13–16).

## Source-quality notes

- **Primary:** the two 1604 broadsides (IA scans of EEBO microfilm, rows 1, 3, 4, 7, 13, 14). The OCR is poor. It supports wording, not exact spelling. Anyone quoting the original spelling should read the scan image (the PDF in each IA item). The king's copy is noted "Stained. Copy from HN spliced at end."
- **Primary transcription:** Ellis 1843 (Camden Society). He transcribed the BM sheet and modernised u/v and i/j. It is the best readable text of the petition and the source of the date and place heading. Ellis's headnote is his own opinion ("vain and ambitious", "occasional impostor"). Use it as 1843 opinion only.
- **Early biography:** Thomas Smith, *Vita Joannis Dee* in *Vitae* (1707). The DNB lists Smith's Latin life among its "principal authorities". Smith wrote about a century after the events; what papers he used was not checked this session. His Salisbury sentence is hedged ("ut videtur") and gives no document in the passage I read. The OCR reads long-s as f. My translation of row 10 is a paraphrase.
- **Victorian reference:** *Athenae Cantabrigienses* (C. H. and Thompson Cooper) and the DNB (Thompson Cooper). They share an author, and the DNB wording follows Athenae closely. Treat them as **one** witness.
- **Early 20th-century biography:** Charlotte Fell Smith (1909). She is sympathetic to Dee and her language is emotive. Her statement that the Commons appeal asked for an "Act Generall against slander" is useful but should be checked against the broadside margin.
- **Modern scholarship:** Parry (Yale), and in principle ODNB, Clulee, Harkness and Sherman, could not be read this session. None of the conclusions above rest on them.
- **Maps only:** none used.

## What this changes for the pages

- `john-dee` / `james-i`: keep "On 5 June 1604 at Greenwich Dee petitioned James to be tried and cleared of the slander that he was a 'conjurer, or caller, or invocator of divels', offering to die if it were proved." Cite the broadside and Ellis.
- Replace "James, informed by Salisbury, refused" with the wording the record supports: *"No answer from the king is recorded. Dee's earliest biographer, Thomas Smith (1707), says James was informed about Dee's studies by Robert Cecil and, 'as it seems', despised him. The Victorian DNB repeats this as a refusal. Cecil was not yet Earl of Salisbury in June 1604."* Tier: Contested.
- Add the verse appeal to the Commons (8 June 1604) as a separate document. Do not merge it with the petition to the king, whose own text separately asks for trial before Parliament.
