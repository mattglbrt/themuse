# Lives of William Tyndale, Erasmus and Benjamin Blayney

## The question

From QUEUE.md (Following the Muse): the biographical facts that the `william-tyndale`, `erasmus` and `benjamin-blayney` nodes need.
- **Tyndale:** birth (uncertain), education, the 1526 Worms New Testament, the 1534 revision, the Pentateuch (1530) and Jonah, the arrest at Antwerp (1535) and the execution at Vilvorde (1536: date and manner).
- **Erasmus:** birth (year disputed), death (Basel, 1536), and the five editions of the Greek NT (1516, 1519, 1522, 1527, 1535) with Froben.
- **Blayney:** birth and death, Oxford college, career (Hebrew, the 1769 Oxford Bible, Regius Professor from 1787), and his 1769 report to the Clarendon Press Delegates (Scrivener App. D).

**Already covered elsewhere (not redone):** `2026-09-22-kjv-sources-lineage.md` rows 12-16 and 20 (Erasmus 1516 / Froben / *Novum Instrumentum*, his manuscripts, the Comma, and Tyndale following Erasmus's 1519 and 1522 texts). `2026-09-22-kjv-afterlife.md` rows C3, C7, C8 and C10 (Blayney's 1769 edition, the fire conflict, the pointer to App. D, the 1787 chair). This memo adds second sources to C3 and C10 (rows B6 and B7 below) and reads App. D itself (B9-B12).

## Headline findings

1. **Tyndale's birth is unknown.** The ranges on record are 1490-95 (DNB, EB1911), 1491-94 (Tyndale Society) and a flat "1484" (Mombert, *New Schaff-Herzog*). The node should say "c. 1490-1494, date unknown" and show the spread.
2. **Tyndale's execution day is contested.** The DNB, EB1911 and Mombert give **6 October 1536**. The Tyndale Society says "late September or early October, 1536" and, on another page, "the autumn of 1536". All sources agree on the manner: strangled, then burned. The three sources for 6 October are all older reference works and probably share a source (inferred), so they are not three independent witnesses.
3. **The 1530 Pentateuch imprint is false.** It reads "Marlborow [Marburg]: Hans Lufft", but Cambridge University Library calls it "a fictitious imprint" whose printer was "probably Johannes Hoochstraten of Antwerp". Mombert had already said in the early 1900s that Luft "never had a printing-press at Marburg". Don't write "printed at Marburg" as fact.
4. **Tyndale at Cambridge is not documented.** The DNB says he was there "probably till the close of 1521". The Tyndale Society says it is "possible but seemingly unrecorded in the university archives."
5. **Erasmus's birth year is a real scholarly dispute.** Vredeveld (*Renaissance Quarterly*, 1993) opens by saying the literature has never "given us a firm grip on the year of his birth". He sets 1466 against R. L. DeMolen's case for 1469. The encyclopedias say "probably in 1466". The birthplace is also questioned: a contemporary source says Gouda, while Erasmus styled himself "of Rotterdam" (EB1911).
6. **The five Greek NT edition years** (1516, 1519, 1522, 1527, 1535) are confirmed by Blumell (BYU RSC, 2019) and EB1911 (P. S. Allen). **The printer of editions 2-5 was not verified.** Every fetched source names Froben only for 1516. The node must not say "all five printed by Froben" until a source is found.
7. **Blayney:** 1728-1801. He died 20 Sept. 1801 at Poulshot rectory, Wilts, aged 73. He matriculated at **Worcester College** on 1 July 1746 aged 18, took the B.A. in 1750 and the M.A. in 1753, and was fellow and later vice-principal of **Hertford College**. He was Regius Professor of Hebrew from 1787. The DNB and Foster's *Alumni Oxonienses* agree. **Conflict:** Foster and Scrivener give the chair as "1787-1802", though both sources give his death as 1801. **His exact birth date was not found.**
8. **Blayney's report (Scrivener App. D) has been read in part.** It is addressed "To the Rev. the Vice-Chancellor, and the other Delegates of the Clarendon Press" and signed from "Hertford College, Oct. 25, 1769". He collated the 1611 folio, the 1701 Lloyd edition and two recent Cambridge editions, and he credits "Dr Paris" with prior corrections to the italics.
