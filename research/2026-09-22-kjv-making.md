# Research memo: How the King James Bible was made (companies, rules, base text, 1611 printing, preface)

**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Question (Phase 1, KJV node, Q2 of 8):** How the KJV was made: the six companies (where each sat, who led each, which books each translated); Richard Bancroft's rules for the translators (text of the 15 rules as printed in a named source); the Bishops' Bible as the base text; the 1611 first printing (printer Robert Barker, format, date); and the translators' preface "The Translators to the Reader" (Miles Smith).

**Short answer:**
- **Six companies, two each at Westminster, Oxford and Cambridge.** Two independent sources confirm this: Cambridge University Library's 2011 exhibition captions, and the English delegates' own report to the Synod of Dort (1618; printed in the *Acta*, 1620). The Dort report gives the arrangement as two parts "Theologis quibusdam Londinensibus" and four parts split equally between the two universities.
- **Who translated which books.** The standard list is the one Bishop Burnet printed (*History of the Reformation*) "Ex MS. D. Borlase". Pollard (1911) reprints it and calls it "the most trustworthy" of the surviving lists:
  - Westminster (Hebrew): Genesis to 2 Kings, under the Dean of Westminster (Lancelot Andrewes).
  - Cambridge (Hebrew): 1 Chronicles to Ecclesiastes, led in the list by Edward Lively.
  - Oxford (Hebrew): the Prophets and Lamentations, led by John Harding.
  - Oxford (Greek): the Gospels, Acts and Revelation, under the Dean of Christ Church (Thomas Ravis).
  - Westminster (Greek): the Epistles, under the Dean of Chester (William Barlow).
  - Cambridge: the Prayer of Manasses "and the rest of the Apocrypha", led in the list by John Duport.

  Who headed each company is partly an inference from list order plus Rule 13. Only Andrewes's leadership has a second, independent source.
- **The 15 rules.** Found in full in Pollard 1911, which reprints Burnet (4th ed., 1715). Burnet's heading attributes them to the King: "An Order set down for the Translating of the Bible, by King James". Westcott (1905) says they were drawn up "probably under the direction of Bancroft". So calling them "Bancroft's rules" is a probable attribution, not something the document itself states.
- **Base text.** Rule 1 names "the Bishops Bible" as the base. The Bodleian holds the surviving annotated 1602 Bishops' Bible (Arch. A b.18), printed by Robert Barker. The Bodleian's caption says forty unbound copies of that 1602 edition went to the translators.
- **The 1611 printing.** The title page reads "Imprinted at London by Robert Barker, Printer to the Kings most Excellent Maiestie. Anno Dom. 1611." The book is a folio. **No month or day is documented**: Pollard says "nor have we any information as to the month in which it was issued", and CUL says "at some point in 1611". I could not verify the "May 2, 1611" date that circulates online, and it should not be used.
- **The preface.** "The Translators to the Reader" is unsigned in the 1611 text. Pollard and Westcott both attribute it to Miles Smith.

## The 15 rules, as printed (Burnet, via Pollard 1911)

Source: A. W. Pollard (ed.), *Records of the English Bible* (London: Henry Frowde, 1911), Introduction. The text is reprinted from Gilbert Burnet, *The History of the Reformation of the Church of England*, 4th ed. (London, 1715), Part II, "A Collection of Records", p. 333 ff., where it carries the side-note "Ex MS. D. Borlase". It was read from archive.org OCR, leaves 68–70 of `recordsofenglish00polluoft`. Pollard's printed page numbers were **not captured**; the node author should read them off the scan (https://archive.org/details/recordsofenglish00polluoft) before citing a page. OCR slips are corrected in [brackets]. The text is otherwise as returned.

> The Rules to be observed in the Translation of the Bible.
> 1. The ordinary Bible read in the Church, commonly called the Bishops Bible, to be followed, and as little altered as the Truth of the original will permit.
> 2. The Names of the Prophets, and the Holy Writers, with the other Names of the Text, to be retained, as nigh as may be, accordingly as they were vulgarly used.
> 3. The old Ecclesiastical Words to be kept, viz. the Word Church not to be translated Congregation &c.
> 4. When a Word hath divers Significations, that to be kept which hath been most commonly used by the most of the Ancient Fathers, being agreeable to the Propriety of the Place and the Analogy of the Faith.
> 5. The Division of the Chapters to be altered, either not at all, or as little as may be, if Necessity so require.
> 6. No Marginal Notes at all to be affixed, but only for the Explanation of the Hebrew or Greek Words, which cannot without some circumlocution, so briefly and fitly be express'd in the Text.
> 7. Such Quotations of Places to be marginally set down as shall serve for the fit Reference of one Scripture to another.
> 8. Every particular Man of each Company, to take the same Chapter, or Chapters, and having translated or amended them severally by himself, where he thinketh good, all to meet together, confer what they have done, and agree for their Parts what shall stand.
> 9. As any one Company hath dispatched any one Book in this Manner they shall send it to the rest, to be consider'd of seriously and judiciously, for His Majesty is very careful in this Point.
> 10. If any Company, upon the Review of the Book so sent, doubt or differ upon any Place, to send them Word thereof; note the Place, and withal send the Reasons, to which if they consent not, the Difference to be compounded at the General Meeting, which is to be of the chief Persons of each Company, at the end of the Work.
> 11. When any Place of special Obscurity is doubted of Letters to be directed, by Authority, to send to any Learned Man in the Land, for his Judgement of such a Place.
> 12. Letters to be sent from every Bishop to the rest of his Clergy, admonishing them of this Translation in hand; and to move and charge as many as being skilful in the Tongues; and having taken Pains in that kind, to send his particular Observations to the Company, either at Westminster, Cambridge or Oxford.
> 13. The Directors in each Company, to be the Deans of Westminster and Chester for that Place; and the King's Professors in the Hebrew or Greek in either University.
> 14. These translations to be used when they agree better with the Text than the Bishops Bible. Tindoil's [OCR; Westcott reads "Tindale's"]. Matthews. Coverdale's. Whitchurch's. Geneva.
> 15. Besides the said Directors before mentioned, three or four of the most Ancient and Grave Divines, in either of the Universities, not employed in Translating, to be assigned by the Vice-Chancellor, upon Conference with the rest of the Heads, to be Overseers of the Translations as well Hebrew as Greek, for the better Observation of the 4th Rule above specified.

Cross-check: Westcott 1905 (leaves 135, 137) prints rules 1, 2 (start), 12 (end), 13, 14 and 15 in modernised spelling, also following Burnet (ed. 1681), and they agree in substance. Westcott's rule 14 reads "viz. Tindale's, Matthew's, Coverdale's, Whitchurch's, Geneva." Both are from Burnet, so they are **not** independent witnesses to the rules. The only independent contemporary account of the rules is the Dort report (row 23), which gives a shorter, different set of seven.

"Whitchurch's" means the Great Bible (Edward Whitchurch was one of its printers). This gloss is my inference. It is consistent with the CUL caption, which lists "the Great Bible" where Rule 14 has "Whitchurch's".
