John Fian, also called John Cunningham, was a schoolmaster at Saltpans who was accused in the [[north-berwick-witch-trials|North Berwick witch hunt]], tortured, convicted, and burned. The Muse stops here because the London pamphlet about the hunt was built around him and around the king who heard the confessions, [[james-i|James VI]].

## Historical Context

He was tried in the hunt that the *Survey of Scottish Witchcraft* describes as having "revovled around witchcraft and treason" (spelling as in the Survey; case C/EGD/63), in which King James was "involved as alleged victim, interrogator, judge, and demonologist" (Normand & Roberts 2000, abstract).

## Life & Work

The pamphlet [[newes-from-scotland|Newes from Scotland]] identifies him as "Doctor Fian, alias Iohn Cunningham, maister of the Schoole at Saltpans in Lowthian." Saltpans is Prestonpans. "Doctor" is the pamphlet's title for him.

He was tortured with the device *Newes* calls "the bootes." The *Survey* records his trial, under the name John Fean, on 26 December 1590 in the central court at Edinburgh, with a verdict of "Guilty," and notes that "Newes From Scotland describes extreme toture being used against him - notably the bootes" ("toture" as in the Survey; trial T/LA/928).

*Newes* says he was "burned in the Castle hill of Edenbrough on asaterdaie in the ende of Ianuarie last past. 1591."

## Claims & Disputes

- **Contested: when he died.** The only source for his death on this page is *Newes*, which gives the end of January 1591. The *Survey* records his trial but no execution date. Other dates are given online and they disagree with each other, so none of them is used here.
