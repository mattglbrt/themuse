# Research memo: James VI's *Daemonologie* and the North Berwick witch trials

**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Question (Phase 1, Q8 of 8):** *Daemonologie*: publication date, place, printer (Robert Waldegrave, Edinburgh, 1597?); its form (dialogue, three books); the 1603 London reprints; its documented link to the North Berwick witch trials (1590-91): what happened, the accused (Agnes Sampson, John Fian, etc.), James's personal involvement, the pamphlet *Newes from Scotland* (1591); and what the preface of *Daemonologie* itself says about its motive. Find a digitized primary text and scholarly secondary. Quote the preface exactly from a named edition.

**Short answer:** Confirmed. The 1597 title page reads "Printed by Robert Walde-graue, Printer to the Kings Majestie. An. 1597." The title page names no place. Catalogers supply Edinburgh, and a 1603 London reprint says it was printed "according to the copie printed at Edenburgh". The book is a dialogue in three books between Philomathes and Epistemon. At least two London editions appeared in 1603 (STC 14365 and STC 14366). The preface gives its motive as "The fearefull aboundinge at this time in this countrie" of witches, and it attacks Scot and Wierus by name. The link to North Berwick is established. Normand & Roberts (2000) describe James as "alleged victim, interrogator, judge, and demonologist" in that witch hunt. *Newes from Scotland* has Agnes Sampson examined "before the Kings Maiestie" at Holyrood. The Survey of Scottish Witchcraft lists "James (VI) Stewart" as an investigator in her case. I did **not** verify that the preface itself mentions North Berwick. See the open items.

## Source-quality notes

- **Primary documents.** Gutenberg #25929 is a transcription of the 1597 text. It does not name a source copy, so cite it as "Project Gutenberg eBook #25929, transcribing the 1597 edition". The HTML version says "Most recently updated: May 21, 2023" and the .txt version says June 20, 2020. Cite the release date (2008-06-29) and give the URL. For the preface, Gutenberg is the better text to quote. Harrison states his reprint is line-for-line for "The text (not the Preface)", so his preface may not follow the 1597 lineation exactly. EEBO-TCP A00710 is the scholarly-standard transcription of *Newes from Scotland* (Bodleian copy, STC 10841a) and is the best primary text for it.
- **Scholarly secondary.** The Survey of Scottish Witchcraft (Goodare, Martin, Miller, Yeoman, Edinburgh, 2003) is a peer-built academic database. It cites the Books of Adjournal (JC2/2), Pitcairn, and the Calendar of State Papers (Scotland) v. 10. Normand & Roberts (Exeter 2000) is the standard scholarly edition of the North Berwick documents, *Newes*, and *Demonology*. I saw only its abstracts. The full text is paywalled on Liverpool Scholarship Online, and archive.org has a controlled-lending copy (https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780859893886) that I did not open.
- **Catalogs** (Wellcome, UW-Madison, Open Library) are reliable for imprints but derive from STC/ESTC. Rows 13 and 14 are not independent of each other. Rows 2, 3 and 4 are independent of Gutenberg.
- **How to handle *Newes* content.** Its narrative is a propaganda pamphlet (Normand & Roberts, row 26) built from confessions extracted under torture. The pamphlet itself names the pilliwinks and the boots. The site should present it as what the pamphlet reports. The Devil at the kirk and the christened cat are confession content, not events. What the site can state flatly is that the confessions were made, by named people, under documented torture, in front of the king.
- **Wikipedia** (Daemonologie, North Berwick witch trials, John Fian) was used only as a map. It conflicts with itself: the North Berwick article gives Fian's burning as "December 16, 1590", while the John Fian article gives 27 January 1591. *Newes* says the end of January 1591, and the Survey has his trial on 26/12/1590 with no execution date. Do not use the Wikipedia dates.
