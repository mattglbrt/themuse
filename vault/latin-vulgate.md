The Latin Vulgate wasn't the King James translators' base text, since their preface says they worked from "the Hebrew text of the Olde Testament, the Greeke of the New." It got into the King James Bible by side doors anyway: [[erasmus|Erasmus]] translated the last six verses of Revelation into Greek from the Vulgate, Scrivener (1881) found places where the Authorised Version "exactly follows the Latin Vulgate," and James G. Carleton (1902) traced Vulgate words that came in through the Catholic [[douay-rheims|Rheims New Testament]]. In the Apocrypha, 2 Esdras and the Prayer of Manasses were translated from the Latin, because 2 Esdras "exists only in the common Latin version" and "the Greek was first published in Walton's Polyglott (1657)" for the Prayer (Scrivener 1884).

## Where the Muse Leads

- [[erasmus]]: who filled the gap at the end of Revelation, and Acts 9:6, from the Vulgate.
- [[textus-receptus]]: the Greek line that kept those Vulgate readings.
- [[douay-rheims]]: the Catholic English New Testament that, by Carleton's account, passed Vulgate words on to the King James.
- [[translators-to-the-reader]]: the preface that names the Hebrew and Greek as the translators' texts, and lists Latin among the aids they consulted.
