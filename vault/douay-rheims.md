The Rheims New Testament of 1582 was a Roman Catholic English translation, and it isn't among the versions the King James translators' rules told them to consult. James G. Carleton argued in 1902 that the King James New Testament borrowed from it anyway, many words from the Vulgate among them: "yet with respect to the distinctive touches which the Authorized New Testament has derived from the earlier translations, her debt to Roman Catholic Rheims is hardly inferior to her debt to puritan Geneva." The size of that debt is Carleton's judgment.

## Where the Muse Leads

- [[geneva-bible]]: the puritan Bible Carleton sets beside Rheims as a comparable source.
- [[latin-vulgate]]: the Latin behind many of the words Carleton says came through Rheims.
- [[king-james-bible]]: the Protestant Bible that, on Carleton's reading, borrowed from the Catholic one.
