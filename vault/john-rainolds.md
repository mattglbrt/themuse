John Rainolds (1549-1607; Barlow spells him "Reynalds," others "Reynolds") was President of Corpus Christi College, Oxford, the leader of the puritan side at the [[hampton-court-conference|Hampton Court Conference]] in January 1604, and the man who asked [[james-i|James I]] for a new translation of the Bible. The Muse stops here because the [[king-james-bible]] starts with his request. He then sat on one of the companies that made it, and died before it was finished.

## Historical Context

The conference answered the puritan [[millenary-petition]] of 1603. Four ministers argued the puritan case, and Barlow's account of the second day names them: Reynolds (Rainolds), Sparkes, Knewstubs, and Chaderton, with "D. Reynalds the Foreman" (Barlow 1604). The old *Dictionary of National Biography* agrees that "he was expressly called their 'foreman.'" Cambridge University Library's 2011 exhibition calls him "John Reynolds, President of Corpus Christi College, Oxford."

## Life & Work

**Birth.** Thomas Fowler's DNB article says he was "born at Pinhoe, near Exeter, 'about Michaelmas Day,' 1549, was fifth son of Richard Rainolds." Michaelmas is 29 September. The year is confirmed by the title of Mordechai Feingold's modern *Oxford DNB* entry, "Rainolds [Reynolds], John (1549–1607), theologian and college head." The birthplace rests on the DNB alone.

**Corpus Christi College.** The DNB: "on 29 April 1563 he was elected to a scholarship at Corpus." He "became probationary fellow on 11 Oct. 1566, and full fellow two years subsequently," and "On 15 Oct. 1568 he graduated B.A." Joseph Foster's *Alumni Oxonienses* agrees (OCR of the 1891 printing): "scholar Corpus Christi Coll. 1563. fellow 1566, B.A. 15 Oct., 1568". The DNB adds that "He was appointed to what was at that time the important college office of Greek reader in 1572–3." (That appointment rests on the DNB alone.)

**Dean of Lincoln, 1593-1598.** The DNB says "Rainolds was made dean of Lincoln on 10 Dec. 1593." The *Fasti Ecclesiae Anglicanae* records him elected on that day and instituted on 28 March 1594. Foster's "dean of Lincoln 1594-9" counts from the institution, so the sources aren't really at odds. In 1598 he gave up the deanery in an exchange: the *Fasti* says it was agreed "by 8 Nov. with William Cole for presidency of Corpus Christi College Oxford."

**President of Corpus, 1598-1607.** The DNB says "Rainolds was elected on 11 Dec. following," a day found in the DNB only. Foster: "president of his college 1598, until his death si May, 1607" (OCR; "si" is 21).

**The request.** On the second day at Hampton Court, as Barlow records it:

> "After that, he moued his Maiestie, that there might bee a newe translation of the Bible, because, those which were allowed in the raignes of Henrie the eight, and Edward the sixt, were corrupt and not aunswerable to the truth of the Originall." (Barlow 1604)

He gave three examples, from Gal. 4:25, Ps. 105:28, and Ps. 106:30. The second: "Secondly, Psalme, 105. 28. they were not obedient; The Originall beeing, They were not disobedient." Barlow dismissed them as "triuiall and old, and alreadie, in print, often aunswerred." [[richard-bancroft|Bancroft]] objected that "there would be no ende of translating," but James took the idea up.

Accounts of how much weight the proposal carried differ. The Cambridge exhibition says "The notion of a new Bible translation was introduced almost in passing by John Reynolds." The DNB article on Bancroft calls it "a well-sustained proposal for a new translation of the Bible." Either way, an encyclopedia article on the conference hosted by the Christian Classics Ethereal Library says "Reynolds' request for anew translation of the Bible bore fruit in the so-called Authorized Version" (as printed).

**The translation.** Rainolds was a translator as well as the proposer. The DNB: "The company on which he was engaged was that for translating the Prophets. It met in Oxford." On the standard list, printed by Burnet and reprinted by Pollard in 1911, that company reads: "Doctor Harding Dr. Reynolds Dr. Holland Dr. Kilbye Mr. Smith Mr. Brett Mr. Fairclough." It had the four greater prophets with Lamentations, and the twelve lesser prophets (Pollard 1911). The DNB sums up his part: "Rainolds may be said to have initiated the project, and he occupied a leading position among the translators."

**Death.** He didn't live to see it finished. The DNB: "He died on 21 May 1607, when he was not yet fifty-eight," and "he was buried in the college chapel." Foster gives the same day and burial, with a different age (see below). The sources checked don't say where he died.

## Claims & Disputes

- **Established:** Rainolds proposed the new translation at Hampton Court (Barlow 1604; DNB; Cambridge University Library 2011).
- **Sources differ (emphasis):** on whether the proposal was a side remark ("almost in passing," CUL 2011) or a considered case ("well-sustained," DNB on Bancroft). The translators' own 1611 preface frames the puritans' request unkindly, as something raised when their other grounds failed; see [[translators-to-the-reader]].
- **Contested (minor): his age at death.** The DNB says he was "not yet fifty-eight," which fits a birth at Michaelmas 1549. Foster says "aged 58, buried in the college chapel" (OCR). The two haven't been reconciled.
- **Not found:** any link between Rainolds and Hermetic or Cabalist learning. The DNB article on him has no mention of cabala, Hermes, magic, the occult, astrology, or John Dee. That is an absence in one old reference work, not proof of anything, and the modern *Oxford Dictionary of National Biography* hasn't been checked.
