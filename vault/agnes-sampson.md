Agnes Sampson, of Haddington, was one of the people accused in the [[north-berwick-witch-trials|North Berwick witch hunt]]. She was questioned by [[james-i|King James VI]] in person, convicted, and strangled and burned on Edinburgh's Castle Hill on 28 January 1591. The Muse stops here because her examination is the clearest record we have of the king acting as interrogator, the same king whose name is on the [[king-james-bible]].

## Historical Context

She was caught up in what the *Survey of Scottish Witchcraft* calls "the North Berwick hunt, that revovled around witchcraft and treason" (spelling as in the Survey; case C/EGD/63). The king was, in the words of Normand and Roberts, "involved as alleged victim, interrogator, judge, and demonologist" (2000, abstract).

## Life & Work

Almost everything known about her comes from her accusers and her trial. The London pamphlet [[newes-from-scotland|Newes from Scotland]] says she was named by [[geillis-duncan|Geillis Duncan]], a servant who had been tortured, and it calls her "Agnis Sampson the eldest Witch of them al, dwelling in Haddington." That is the pamphlet's label for a woman it was accusing.

According to *Newes*, she "was taken and brought to Haliriud house before the Kings Maiestie and sundry other of the nobility of Scotland, where she was straitly examined." The *Survey* lists "James (VI) Stewart" among the investigators in her case and records that "She confessed to these things in front of King James VI."

The *Survey* records her trial on 21 January 1591 in the central court at Edinburgh, a verdict of "Guilty," and her execution on 28 January 1591 at "Castle Hill." The method is recorded as "Strangle & Burn" (trial T/LA/934). The Survey spells her name Sampsoune.

## Claims & Disputes

- **Established, as the pamphlet's claim only:** *Newes* reports that when the king called the accused "extreame lyars," she took him aside and "declared vnto him the verye woordes which passed betweene the Kings Maiestie and his Queene at Upslo in Norway the first night of their mariage." The pamphlet says this happened. Nothing can confirm it. It is the most famous story about her, and it comes from a propaganda pamphlet reporting an interrogation.
